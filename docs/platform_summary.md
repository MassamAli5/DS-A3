This project asks how well bulk ∑REY in 29 samples from the Early Cambrian Kakul Garlaniyan phosphorites (published supplementary data from my paper) can be predicted from 35 non-REY trace elements. Because ∑REY is exactly the sum of the REY columns, those columns and their ratios were excluded as leakage, together with major oxides (recorded for only 14 samples) and below-detection columns. I froze a layer-stratified split (23 training, 6 test samples, seed 42) and tuned a naive mean baseline, OLS, ridge and lasso with repeated cross-validation on the training samples only. By the rule fixed beforehand (lowest CV RMSE), lasso was selected: CV RMSE 52 ppm against 181 ppm for the mean, and test RMSE 82 ppm (bootstrap 95% interval 32 to 118) on only 6 samples. Ridge scored better on the test set (43 ppm) but was not chosen, since the test set is not used for selection and the intervals overlap. Diagnostics show no clear heteroscedasticity (low power), a calibration slope of 1.07 (95% interval 0.92 to 1.21), and the largest error when layer LPL is held out (102 ppm). The test set contains no chert, and the most-selected predictor (Be) is strongly correlated with others, so coefficients are descriptive, not causal. Weak results are kept: OLS on all predictors interpolates the training data and generalises worse. The analysis is retrospective and limited to this section. Code, results, a PDF report and the AI-use log are in the repository.
